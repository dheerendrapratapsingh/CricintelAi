01 • Motivation & Challenge

The Cricket News Fragmentation Crisis

Sports media coverage produces massive information redundancy, repetitive wire quotes, and fragmented coverage across outlets.

📰 Multi-Outlet Redundancy

When major events occur (e.g. World Cup squad announcements or IPL auction rules), outlets like ESPNcricinfo, BBC Sport, Cricbuzz, and Reuters publish separate articles containing 70% to 85% duplicate wire quotes.

🧩 Headline & Semantic Divergence

Outlets report the same story using completely distinct editorial phrasing. Lexical keyword matching fails; systems need deep semantic embeddings to recognize topical equivalence.

⏱️ Information Overload

Analysts, fans, and journalists must read dozens of disjointed articles to piece together a chronological timeline and key decisions, causing high cognitive friction.

Our Core Research Objective: Build an unsupervised, multi-tier NLP pipeline that autonomously groups raw news into discrete story events, deduplicates cross-source quotes, and generates an executive briefing with verifiable provenance citations and ~84% information compression.
The primary motivation behind CricIntel AI is addressing sports journalism fragmentation. During any major event—like the T20 World Cup selection—every media house reports the exact same BCCI press release. A user is forced to read 6 articles with identical quotes. Traditional search engines only retrieve articles; they don't synthesize them. CricIntel AI transforms unstructured feeds into structured intelligence briefings.

04 • Natural Language Processing

Dual-Tier Semantic Vector Embeddings

Deep transformer contextual embeddings backed by mathematical Latent Semantic Analysis.

🌟 Primary: SentenceTransformers

We leverage all-MiniLM-L6-v2 to map each article into a dense 384-dimensional vector space. It captures deep contextual nuances, synonyms (e.g. "skipper" == "captain"), and event associations.

# S-BERT Embedding with L2 Normalization
embeddings = model.encode(
    texts,
    normalize_embeddings=True
) # shape: (N, 384)

⚡ Secondary: TF-IDF + TruncatedSVD (LSA)

Instant, 100% dependency-free mathematical fallback. Computes sublinear TF-IDF with (1,2)-grams, projects via TruncatedSVD down to 48 latent dimensions, and applies $L_2$ unit normalization.

# LSA Dense Projection Fallback
tfidf = TfidfVectorizer(ngram_range=(1,2), sublinear_tf=True)
svd = TruncatedSVD(n_components=48, random_state=42)
embeddings = normalize(svd.fit_transform(tfidf))
Why L2 Unit Normalization Matters: When vectors have unit length ($\|v\|_2 = 1$), the inner dot product equals cosine similarity ($u \cdot v = \cos \theta$). This accelerates clustering distance computation and prevents article length from biasing cluster assignment.
For vector representation, we implemented a dual-tier approach. Primary tier: MiniLM SentenceTransformers yielding 384 dimensions. To ensure 100% reliability during viva evaluations where internet or PyTorch might be constrained, our second tier uses TF-IDF with TruncatedSVD, mathematically known as Latent Semantic Analysis. Both outputs are L2 normalized, ensuring cosine distance is strictly invariant to article word length.
05 • Unsupervised Learning

Unsupervised Semantic Story Clustering

Grouping evolving dispatches into story events without prior knowledge of $k$.

🌳 Agglomerative Hierarchical (Default)

Cosine Distance + Average Linkage

Ideal for streaming news. Rather than guessing $k$, we set a cosine distance cutoff (0.65). Articles merge only if their semantic angle is within threshold. Achieved highest Silhouette score (0.542).

🎯 K-Means Partitional

Fixed Cluster Cardinality

Clusters articles around $k$ centroids in Euclidean vector space. Fast and deterministic, achieving Silhouette 0.518, but requires knowing $k$ in advance.

🔍 DBSCAN Density-Based

Outlier & Noise Isolation

Identifies dense neighborhoods with $\epsilon = 0.45$. Automatically marks isolated articles as noise (cluster -1) rather than forcing them into invalid story groups.

🏷️ Automatic Semantic Story Titling

Our clusterer automatically generates human-readable story titles by combining the dominant tournament entity, the most frequent player entities, and high-frequency title tokens (e.g. "ICC Men's T20 World Cup — Rohit Sharma, Hardik Pandya").

In news intelligence, we don't know how many events will break on a given day, making K-Means sub-optimal. Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering with average linkage and cosine distance is our recommended default. Articles merge hierarchically, and we slice the dendrogram at a distance cutoff of 0.65. Furthermore, our auto-titling algorithm names each cluster based on identified player and tournament entities.
06 • Text Summarization

Multi-Document Briefing & MMR Synthesizer

Eliminating cross-document quote redundancy while maximizing information diversity.

🧮 Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) Formula

MMR iteratively selects sentences balancing story centrality against redundancy with already selected sentences:

MMR = argmax [ λ · Sim(S_i, Centroid) 
               - (1 - λ) · max Sim(S_i, S_j) ]
where λ = 0.65

This explicitly prevents duplicate quotes (e.g., Rohit Sharma's press conference remarks) from being repeated in the briefing.

🕸️ Graph-Based TextRank (Eigenvector Power)

Constructs a complete sentence similarity graph where edges are cosine similarities between sentence vectors. Runs PageRank power iteration to determine sentence graph centrality:

PR(S_i) = (1 - d) + d · Σ [ W_ji / Σ W_jk · PR(S_j) ]
where damping factor d = 0.85
