1. Executive Abstract & Problem Statement

Sports news coverage is characterized by intense fragmentation and cross-outlet redundancy. When major events unfold—such as ICC World Cup squad announcements, IPL auction guidelines, or historic Ashes finishes—dozens of news portals (ESPNcricinfo, BBC Sport, Cricbuzz, Reuters, Wisden, Indian Express) publish individual dispatches quoting the identical press conference statements verbatim. Simultaneously, editorial variations in headlines prevent lexical keyword systems from identifying topical equivalence.

CricIntel AI is an end-to-end unsupervised natural language processing intelligence system that automates the sports intelligence cycle:

  • Unsupervised Event Clustering: Groups un-labeled news articles into cohesive real-world story events using Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering with Cosine Distance.
  • Cross-Document Redundancy Elimination: Deploys Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) and Graph-based TextRank to synthesize briefings that eliminate duplicate quotes while preserving divergent perspectives.
  • Source Attribution & Traceability: Retains complete provenance for every summary sentence, providing clickable links to original journalists and publishers.
  • Interactive Visual Analytics: Renders real-time macro trends (tournament proportions, player mention frequencies, publication timelines) on a Streamlit dashboard.

3. System Design & Architectural Pipeline

The architecture is decoupled into five sequential, fault-tolerant processing layers:

  1. Ingestion & Domain Preprocessing: Strips markup via BeautifulSoup, cleans news wire prefixes (e.g. "LONDON (Reuters) —"), applies regex sentence splitting preserving bowling figures (e.g. "5-28"), and extracts cricket player, team, and tournament entities.
  2. Semantic Vector Space: Primary: SentenceTransformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) generating 384-dimensional dense vectors. Secondary fallback: TfidfVectorizer with TruncatedSVD (LSA 48D) and $L_2$ unit normalization.
  3. Story Clustering Engine: Evaluates pairwise cosine distances ($1 - \cos\theta$). Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering merges articles under average linkage with a dynamic cutoff ($\tau = 0.65$), automatically discovering natural event boundaries without prior knowledge of $k$.
  4. Multi-Article Summarizer: Applies Graph TextRank (eigenvector power iteration) and Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) to balance story centrality against redundancy with already chosen sentences.
  5. Streamlit Dashboard & Export: Interactive dark pitch theme with 5 tabs, real-time hyperparameter sliders, Plotly analytics, and one-click JSON/CSV export.

6. Mathematical Formulations & Core Source Code

MMR = argmax [ λ • Sim(S_i, Centroid) - (1 - λ) • max Sim(S_i, S_j) ]   (where λ = 0.65) 
# cricintel/summarization/extractive.py — Maximal Marginal Relevance Implementation
def select_sentences_mmr(sentences, sentence_vectors, centroid, top_k=4, lambda_param=0.65):
    selected_indices = []
    unselected = list(range(len(sentences)))
    sims_to_centroid = cosine_similarity(sentence_vectors, centroid.reshape(1, -1)).flatten()
    first_idx = int(np.argmax(sims_to_centroid))
    selected_indices.append(first_idx)
    unselected.remove(first_idx)
    while len(selected_indices) < top_k and unselected:
        redundancies = cosine_similarity(sentence_vectors[unselected], sentence_vectors[selected_indices])
        max_redundancy = np.max(redundancies, axis=1)
        mmr_scores = lambda_param * sims_to_centroid[unselected] - (1 - lambda_param) * max_redundancy
        best_unselected_idx = np.argmax(mmr_scores)
        chosen_idx = unselected[best_unselected_idx]
        selected_indices.append(chosen_idx)
        unselected.remove(chosen_idx)
    return [sentences[i] for i in selected_indices]

7. Viva Voce Defense Guide for Dr. Gundimeda Venugopal

Q1: Why did you choose Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering over K-Means?

Answer: In real-time news streams, the number of breaking events $k$ cannot be predicted in advance. K-Means assumes spherical equal-radius clusters in Euclidean space and forces unrelated articles into partitions. Agglomerative Clustering uses Cosine Distance ($1 - \cos\theta$), invariant to article length, and merges articles hierarchically until the distance cutoff (0.65) is reached.

Q2: How does your system eliminate wire quote redundancy across news agencies?

Answer: When multiple agencies report on a BCCI press conference, they publish identical quotes. CricIntel deploys Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) with $\lambda = 0.65$. The penalty term $(1 - \lambda) \max \text{Sim}(S_i, S_j)$ directly suppresses near-duplicate sentences, ensuring each selected bullet point adds novel factual information.

Q3: How is source provenance guaranteed in your executive briefings?

Answer: Each sentence retains metadata linking back to article ID, publication outlet, author, and timestamp. The UI renders both a chronological reporting timeline and a clickable citation list.
